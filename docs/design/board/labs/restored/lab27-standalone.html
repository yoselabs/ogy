How to read this board

Goals, capabilities, workflows

Goal: a result the product exists to reach (yellow note). Capability: something the product can do to get there (orange note). Workflow: how one result is actually reached, step by step (a frame). A workflow realizes a capability, and a capability realizes a goal. Each one lives in an area (a topic: review, checks, capture...), but links can cross areas, so a goal in one area can be served by capabilities of another.

Inside a frame

Each little window is a step. A blue button is something the person can choose; a grey box is a condition the product itself decides on. The arrow starts at the button and shows where it leads. Green ✓ is an ending that reached the result, red ✗ an ending that failed or was refused.

One workflow calling another

A step can run a whole other workflow midway: the purple dashed arrow leaves that step for the called workflow. When the called one ends, its endings come back as dotted purple arrows into the conditions of the calling step, so the caller carries on according to how the call ended (clean, blocked...).

Lines

A solid coloured line starts at a button (the dot) and ends at the screen it leads to. Buttons that lead to the same place share one line. A thin grey line links two things, for example a workflow to the thing it changes. A dashed purple line is a step running another workflow midway. A dotted purple line is that workflow's ending coming back into the calling step. Amber and teal lines appear when you select something: where it leads and what leads to it.

Small tags inside a button or condition

A blue-grey tag with an eye shape in its meaning is something the rule looks at (hover it to read what kind of thing it is). A green tag is something that happens when this branch is taken: an envelope tells someone, a plus-document creates a thing, a pencil changes one, a bin deletes one, an arrow moves a thing to another state, for example loan open → returned. When you select a workflow, the things it looks at get a dotted line, the things it changes a solid one.

Who starts a workflow

The small pill under the description says what starts it: a person, an event (such as a loan being returned), a schedule, or in the background, meaning another workflow calls it and no person ever sees it.

Who decides

Blue buttons are a person's choices. Violet buttons are an AI's. Grey boxes are the product deciding by a rule; you pick the one you want to try. A line starting "Looks at" says what data the rule reads. A green line says what the choice changes.

Icons

Moving around

Scrolling (wheel or two fingers) zooms. Hold the middle button, or drag the empty background, to pan; Shift+scroll pans sideways. Click selects. You can drag a block with the left button to read under it; it springs back when you let go. Double-click a workflow to focus on it: everything unrelated disappears.
