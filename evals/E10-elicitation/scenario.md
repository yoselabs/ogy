# Scenario: neighbourhood tool library (owner's words, transcribed)

[1] So, we run a tool library for the neighbourhood. [2] Think of it like a book library, but for drills, ladders, saws, a pressure washer, that kind of thing. [3] We have about four hundred members right now. [4] Everything happens in two places: the app, and the counter at the shed. [5] The shed is open Tuesday and Thursday evenings and Saturday mornings, and the counter is staffed by volunteers.

[6] There are two plans. [7] Basic is a yearly fee, and you can borrow hand tools. [8] Plus costs more per year, and you can also borrow power tools, and your loans are longer. [9] Basic loans are one week, plus loans are two weeks. [10] A basic member can have three tools out at once, a plus member five. [11] People join in the app and pay the yearly fee through Stripe, and it renews automatically every year. [12] You can upgrade from basic to plus whenever you like.

[13] How borrowing works. [14] You open the catalog in the app, find the tool, and reserve it for a pickup day, so one of the days the shed is open. [15] Then you come to the counter on that day, the volunteer scans the QR sticker on the tool and your member card, and the loan starts. [16] The due date is just the pickup day plus your loan length. [17] Power tools need a deposit; we charge it on Stripe when the loan starts, and when the tool comes back fine, the deposit goes back to you. [18] Also, for power tools you must have done the safety intro. [19] Marta, she's our fixer, runs a safety intro once a month, and after you've done it, we mark it on your profile. [20] And no power tools for anyone under eighteen, obviously.

[21] If the tool you want is out, you can join the waitlist for it. [22] When it comes back, the next person on the waitlist gets an email, and they have a while to claim it. [23] All our emails go out through Postmark, by the way.

[24] Returns. [25] You bring the tool back to the counter during opening hours. [26] The volunteer looks it over. [27] If it's fine, it goes back on the shelf and the deposit is released. [28] If it's damaged, it goes to Marta, she looks at it and fixes it, and depending on what happened, the member might pay for the repair. [29] While it's with Marta it's in repair, so nobody can reserve it.

[30] We send a reminder email the day before a tool is due. [31] If you're late, there's a late fee for every day, and if you're late for too long, we block you from borrowing anything until you bring it back and pay. [32] And if a tool isn't back a month after it was due, we treat it as lost and we charge you the replacement.

[33] After you return a tool, the app asks you to rate it, one to five stars. [34] People like doing that.

[35] Donations are a big part of how we grow. [36] People drop off tools they don't need. [37] A volunteer inspects the tool, and if it's good, adds it to the catalog: name, category, photo, and whether it's a power tool. [38] It gets a QR sticker.

[39] A tool can be available, reserved, on loan, in repair, lost, or retired. [40] We retire tools when they're beyond fixing.

[41] Volunteers sign up for shifts in a Google Sheet that our coordinator, Sam, keeps. [42] When a volunteer opens the counter app at the start of a shift, it records who's on.

[43] Every month the board wants a report: the most borrowed tools, how many loans were late, and the money we took in, fees, deposits kept, memberships.

[44] That's mostly it. [45] We want it simple. [46] Most of our volunteers aren't technical, so the counter app has to be dead easy.
